Let's get your first ride in

Three steps. After that, every ride shows up here with a short report.

  1. Connect your logger
    Turn on your phone's hotspot, then switch the logger on. It joins your hotspot by itself. Flip the toggle to record a ride, flip it back to stop.
  2. Get your rides
    With the hotspot on, finished rides upload by themselves and appear here. Next to the logger without signal? Get them straight from it.
  3. Or open a ride file
    Have a CSV from the logger's SD card? Open it from this phone.

    About lining up
        
    No two runs take exactly the same time, so the analyzer matches the same spot on the trail in both rides. Timing is checked a second, independent way; if the rides can't be matched reliably, you're told instead of shown made-up numbers.

            Frame IMU (front vs rear harshness)
            
      
            
        
            
        
            
        
            
        
            
      
            
      
            Measure the IMU distance level with the ground, from the front axle back to the IMU (mid-downtube is about 450–550 mm). Auto-detect finds which way the IMU faces; set it by hand if it says it can't tell. The X/Y/Z arrows are printed on the IMU board.
            
      
            A travel sensor (potentiometer, or “pot”) that isn't plugged in is left out of the numbers, the ride report and lining up rides. Auto-detect spots an unplugged one.

            About the numbers
      
            Positive velocity is compression everywhere. Results differ from the old SD-card analyzer, whose velocities were too high.
            
      
            Compare matches the same spot on the trail in both rides, so every number compares like with like. Each comparison is checked a second, independent way; if the rides can't be matched reliably, you're told instead of shown made-up numbers. Compare map: blue where A was faster, orange where B was, grey where they were even; stops are drawn dark grey and left out. Settings counted in rows mean rows at 250 a second and are scaled for files at other rates, so they always cover the same time.
